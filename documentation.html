01 · SOC Environment Summary

Phase 1 — Objectives 1–2

What is Security Onion used for?

Security Onion is a network security monitoring platform used to collect, index and analyze logs and alerts, including Suricata IDS alerts, Zeek logs and PCAP evidence, to detect and investigate suspicious activity.

Role in the Project

The role in this project was SOC analyst, performing alert triage and incident investigation using Security Onion, Zeek, Suricata and Wireshark.

Evidence Being Analyzed

Security Onion alerts generated against host 192.168.194.129. The environment reported 42 alerts across 7 alert groups over the last 30 days.

Activities Not Allowed

  • Unauthorized scanning of internal database services.
  • Unauthorized connection attempts to internal database services.
  • Unauthorized external IP-lookup or DNS activity.

10 · Executive Summary

Management-level incident summary

On 21 July 2026 at 11:58:14 UTC, Security Onion generated a Suricata alert involving 192.168.194.129.

The investigation reviewed Security Onion alerts, Zeek logs, Suricata alerts and available Wireshark PCAP evidence.

Based on the available evidence, the activity was classified as suspicious and requires further review.

The main concern was repeated targeting of internal database service ports 1433, 1521, 3306 and 5432, indicating possible database-port scanning.

A .cfd DNS query was also observed, although detailed DNS evidence was not provided.

Recommended actions include investigating 192.168.194.129, reviewing Zeek conn.log and endpoint/DNS logs, and restricting unauthorized access to database ports.

Final Severity: MEDIUM

Confidence Level: Medium

2. Investigation Summary

The investigation began with an ET SCAN alert for a TCP connection from 192.168.194.129 to 192.168.194.1 on MSSQL port 1433.

Additional Suricata alerts showed activity targeting Oracle 1521, MySQL 3306 and PostgreSQL 5432, forming a suspicious database-port targeting pattern.

Security Onion reported 42 alerts across 7 alert groups over 30 days. Zeek and full PCAP details were incomplete, so the investigation remains open pending further endpoint and network evidence.

The available evidence supports a suspicious classification rather than a confirmed malicious compromise.

No confirmed successful database access or compromise was established from the supplied evidence.

4–5. Timeline and IOC Summary

Timeline: 21 July 2026 — MSSQL 1433 alert at 11:58:14 UTC, followed by alerts for Oracle 1521, PostgreSQL 5432 and MySQL 3306, plus a reported DNS query to a .cfd TLD.

Primary IOC: Source host/IP 192.168.194.129, associated with repeated database-port scan alerts.

Target IOC: Internal gateway 192.168.194.1, with database service ports 1433, 1521, 3306 and 5432 targeted.

Additional IOC: .cfd TLD DNS activity, Suricata SID 2065867. Exact domain and packet details were not provided.

6. Analysis

The evidence indicates that 192.168.194.129 generated or was associated with multiple connections targeting database service ports on 192.168.194.1.

The first documented event was the MSSQL port 1433 connection at 11:58:14 UTC. Suricata then reported similar activity against ports 1521, 5432 and 3306.

The repeated targeting of different database ports is consistent with possible port scanning or probing. The connection was allowed, meaning the activity was not blocked by the control that generated the alert.

A DNS query to a .cfd TLD was also reported, but the source documentation does not provide the exact domain or complete DNS/PCAP records.

Zeek conn.log was identified as the next investigation pivot, but detailed entries were not supplied.

Therefore, the evidence does not establish whether the activity resulted in successful access or compromise.

Overall, the available evidence supports suspicious scanning activity with medium confidence.

7. Final Classification

The activity is classified as suspicious because multiple Suricata alerts show targeting of restricted database ports from the same host.

The pattern is not sufficient to confirm a compromise, and the available Zeek, PCAP and endpoint evidence is incomplete.

The incident should remain open for further investigation and escalation if additional evidence confirms unauthorized or malicious activity.

8. Impact Assessment

Unauthorized probing of internal database services could expose database ports and reveal services that should not be externally or improperly accessed.

If the activity were successful, it could potentially lead to unauthorized access or further attacks against database services. However, no successful compromise is confirmed by the supplied evidence.

The documented scope is a single host, 192.168.194.129, with medium business impact and medium confidence.

Continued monitoring and correlation with endpoint, DNS, Zeek and PCAP evidence are therefore important.

9. Recommendations

  1. Investigate 192.168.194.129 and confirm the process, user and purpose responsible for the activity.
  2. Review Zeek conn.log and DNS records to validate repeated connections and investigate the .cfd DNS activity.
  3. Restrict unauthorized access to database ports 1433, 1521, 3306 and 5432 and block traffic where appropriate.
  4. Collect endpoint logs and correlate them with Security Onion, Suricata, Zeek and Wireshark evidence. Continue monitoring for recurrence.

10. Lessons Learned

  • Correlate Suricata IDS alerts with Zeek connection/DNS logs and Wireshark PCAP evidence before reaching a final conclusion.
  • Maintain complete network and endpoint evidence so analysts can confirm the source, intent and outcome of suspicious connections.
  • Apply stronger controls to internal database ports and monitor for repeated multi-port scanning patterns.
  • Document investigation timelines, IOCs, severity and confidence consistently so incidents can be escalated and responded to quickly.
